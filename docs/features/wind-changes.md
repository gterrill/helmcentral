# Wind changes

Helmcentral reads the whole ten-day forecast for a turn in the wind and marks it. A change that arrives on Tuesday afternoon then can't hide inside a day card that says "north, 10 knots". It tells you the direction before, the direction after, and when.

- **What counts as a change:** One steady wind gives way to another. A wind counts as steady when it blows from about the same direction (within about 45 degrees) for at least six hours at 5 knots or more. A change is when a steady wind blows from 90 degrees or more away from the wind the last change ended on, and the change is named from that wind, the one that set the pattern. A wind that veers a little at a time is a change once the turn adds up to 90 degrees, and the next steps are judged from there. Up to about twelve hours of light or unsettled air can sit between the two, and it counts as part of the change. The time shown is when the new wind sets in, so a northerly that goes light overnight and comes back southeast at breakfast is marked at breakfast.
- **What doesn't count:** A short burst that comes straight back, a few hours of light and variable air, a single odd hour, and a slow drift that ends less than 90 degrees from where it started. A new wind has to hold for six hours in the forecast before it's shown, so a change in the last few hours of the forecast waits until the forecast can show it holding.
- **How far ahead it reads:** Helmcentral reads the hourly part of the forecast. Some weather sources go from hourly to every three or six hours further out, and a change can't be told from that. Where the hours stop, it says so under the summary and gives the last hour it read and why, for example "Wind changes checked to Thu 19 3 AM: the forecast skips an hour here", or "...: the forecast is no longer hourly" when the gap is longer. If the whole forecast is hourly, nothing is said.
- **How it's named:** By the points of the compass and the time, such as "N→SE 2 PM". Helmcentral doesn't call it a front. The forecast sources don't give hourly pressure, so a trough and a rise can't be shown, and a plain wind change is all that can be said honestly.
- **On the day card:** A small chip under the wind reading shows the first change on that day, or the first one that reaches your marginal wind if any does, and a "+1" says when another follows. If a change can't be read, or falls on a day the forecast has no card for, a line under the summary says so. The day's own wind reading stays where it was.
- **In the summary:** The ten-day summary opens with a wind sentence, for example "Northerlies until a SE change Tue 17 around 2 PM, 15–20 kn behind it." Later changes follow after it. When the forecast holds no change, the sentence isn't there.
- **Louder when it matters:** A chip is muted when the wind after the change stays below your lowest marginal wind, and amber when it reaches it. It is judged on the speed shown: a wind shown as 20 knots is at a 20-knot marginal wind. Marginal comes from your [passage limits](../how-to/set-passage-limits.md): it's 70% of your lowest no-go wind across ahead, abeam and astern. If you haven't set limits, or they can't be read, the forecast still shows, every chip stays muted, and a line under the summary says why: Helmcentral has nothing of yours to judge it against. With no change in the forecast there is nothing to judge, so that line isn't there.
- **How far to trust it:** A change more than about three days out is shown in italics and the summary says it's less certain this far out. Check again as it comes closer.
- **In the watch report:** When a change falls in the forecast at your position, the weather section of a watch report gets a "Wind change" row. It names the first change and says how many follow it. It reads the forecast at the boat's last good position. If your limits can't be read, or you haven't set them, the change is still listed and a note says it wasn't judged against them, and where to set them: "Wind changes not judged against your limits: your passage limits aren't set (Settings → Mate → Passage limits)". If the hourly forecast stops early, it says how far the changes were checked. A forecast isn't a fault on board, so it never raises an alarm.

- **In a passage plan:** The Wind column shows the change instead of the day's average, because an average across a northerly and a south-easterly points at neither. Helmcentral works the changes out for each row when it checks the plan, and the table only shows them. A passage row that holds a change shows the day's speed range with no single direction, since the day had more than one, then the change, for example "NNW→SE 7 AM", then where each wind sits against that leg's course, for example "astern → ahead". A wind that stays on one side reads "stays ahead". Only the changes between your exact departure and your ETA count on a passage row, so a change at 10 AM is left out of a leg that sails at 10.45 AM. A leg that runs overnight counts the change at 2 AM the next morning too, and names the day, for example "N→SE Sun 2 AM". With no ETA, they count from your departure to the end of the day. With no departure time, the whole day counts. For the leg you're on now, the changes count from now to your ETA, and the ETA comes from the departure time you set, so a leg under way with no departure time says its changes weren't checked. The passage row also shows the night you arrive: a change between 6 PM on the day you get there (or your ETA, if that's later) and 6 AM the next morning is listed after the leg's own, at the stop you reach, so a gale rising at 2 AM in the anchorage isn't missed. It shows for a stop you stay at, and for the last stop. A lay day shows a change that falls between 6 PM and 6 AM on the stop's clock, the same hours its wind is read over, without a course, since you're not sailing. With several changes in a row, the first amber one leads, or the first if none is, and "+1 more" counts the rest. Hover over it to see them all. A row with no change reads as it always did, and while the check is still running the cell shows only the current speed range.
- **When a row can't say:** If the forecast for a row stops before its window ends, the Wind cell says "changes not checked" in small grey text, and hovering over it gives the reason. If the forecast ends within the hour after your ETA, that's close enough, and the row doesn't complain. If your passage limits aren't set or can't be read, every row says "changes not checked" and the hover gives that reason, with where to set them: "your passage limits aren't set (Settings → Mate → Passage limits)". Nothing is read for those rows, because without limits there's nothing to judge a change against. If the forecast for one stop can't be read, only the nights at that stop say so, and the others still show their changes. That way a row never looks like a steady day when Helmcentral didn't look.
- **Amber in a plan:** On a passage row the change turns amber when the wind after it reaches the marginal wind for the side it blows from on that leg. A south-easterly on a leg heading south-west is judged against your abeam limit, a westerly against your ahead limit. Ahead is a wind less than 60 degrees off the bow, abeam is from 60 up to 120 degrees, and astern is 120 degrees or more. Marginal is 70% of the no-go wind you set for that side, so it's the same figure the verdict uses. A side with no wind limit set is never amber. A lay day has no course, so it's amber when the change reaches your lowest marginal wind across all three sides, as on the forecast page.

Wind changes read the forecast at the boat's own position, and at each leg's middle in a plan. Mate reads them too: when you ask it about the wind, it lists the changes with their times, says which reach your marginal wind, and tells you if it couldn't judge them against your limits or only read part of the forecast.
